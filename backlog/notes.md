**Decisions worth not relitigating:**
- **The publisher architecture is SETTLED.** The lane provides ORDERING and spans candidate integration + full validation + publication; the short mutex + CAS + ancestry proof + immutable `candidateSha` binding provide CORRECTNESS; the lane is a renewable durable LEASE — no PID probing, signalling, identity nonces, zombie classification, or reaping, ever; validation stays INSIDE the lane turn and OUTSIDE the mutex window; publication worktrees stay fresh/create-only/per-attempt; publisher state stays under FORGE_HOME. **Three separate reds have now tried to move validation out of the lane turn and were wrong every time.**
- **A terminal refusal may NEVER stand over an attempt still recorded `publishing`.** This is the invariant that caught the remote-recovery defect below. Not cosmetic, not a messaging bug.
- **Recovery may fetch into the LOCAL object store; it may NEVER mutate the target.** No push, no update-ref on the remote, no force, no lease consumption.
- **A cancel is terminal and wins.** Recovery never resurrects a cancelled task — but the operator must still be told when a cancelled task's candidate DID land.
- **`MutexLostMidPublishError` is REACHABLE in production** (suspend, SIGSTOP, paused container, swap thrash, long GC/IO stall). Do not close anything by arguing unreachability.
- **The `awaiting_recovery` park deliberately stamps a SHARED `git_state` blocker** while the publication is unresolved (the publish target is shared across campaign items, so an unsettled window genuinely impairs the others). The defect was its LIFETIME, not its kind — it is cleared at the centralized ship transition. Don't "simplify" this into a non-shared park kind.
